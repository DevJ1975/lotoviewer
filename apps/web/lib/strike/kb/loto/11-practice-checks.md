# LOTO 101 practice checks and how to coach them (29 CFR 1910.147(b), (d))

## What the practice checks are

LOTO 101 has four practice checks between its scenes. They are **not graded**: they exist so a worker can try an idea, get it wrong safely, and fix it before the graded knowledge check at the end. Each check has a **Hint** button. The first hint is a nudge; the last one gives part of the answer away. The next scene stays locked until the check is solved.

When a worker asks for help with a practice check, coach in this order:

1. Explain the idea behind the check in one or two sentences.
2. Give one hint, then let them try.
3. Only if they have tried and ask again, give the answer and say why it is right.

The graded knowledge check (the multiple-choice questions at the end) is different: never give or confirm those answers while the worker is taking it.

## Check 1: Match each energy source to the device that isolates it

**The idea.** Every kind of energy has its own way of being physically blocked. An energy-isolating device is a mechanical device that physically prevents the transmission or release of energy (definition in 1910.147(b)).

**Nudges.** Ask: "What would physically stop this energy from reaching the machine?" Electricity flows through wires, so something has to open the circuit. Air and steam flow through pipes, so a valve has to close, and trapped pressure has to be bled. Gravity pulls a raised part down, so something has to hold it up.

**The matches.**
- Electrical → lockable disconnect switch.
- Pneumatic → air valve with lockout and bleed.
- Steam → gate valve with a valve cover lock.
- Gravity → mechanical block or pin.

The scene "Hazardous energy comes in many forms" teaches this.

## Check 2: Tap every true energy-isolation point on the machine

**The idea.** Push buttons, selector switches and other control-circuit devices are **not** energy-isolating devices (1910.147(b)). A control tells the machine what to do; it does not physically cut off the energy. A program, a relay or another person can override a control.

**Nudges.** Ask: "If this device failed or someone pressed the wrong thing, could the energy still get through?" A stop button and a touchscreen send signals. A disconnect and a lockable valve physically break the path.

**The answer.** Two points are correct: the **electrical disconnect** and the **lockable air shutoff valve (with exhaust)**. The **emergency stop button** and the **operator touchscreen** are controls, not isolation points. The scene "Isolate, then lock and tag" teaches this.

## Check 3: Put the seven lockout steps in the right order

**The idea.** The order matters because each step makes the next one safe. You can't isolate energy you haven't identified, and you can't trust a lockout you haven't verified (1910.147(d)).

**Nudges.** Ask: "What do you do before you touch anything?" (You read the placard.) Then: "Who needs to know before the machine stops?" Then: "What is the very last thing you do before you put your hands in?" (You verify.)

**The order.**
1. Prepare: read the placard.
2. Notify affected employees.
3. Shut down with the normal stop.
4. Isolate every energy source.
5. Apply your lock and tag.
6. Release or block stored energy.
7. Verify zero energy.

The scenes "Prepare, notify, shut down", "Isolate, then lock and tag" and "Release stored energy, then verify" teach this.

## Check 4: A coworker says "You're covered, I locked it out"

**The idea.** Each authorized employee who works on the machine puts on their **own** lock and tag (1910.147(f)(3)). Your lock is the only lock that protects you, because only you hold its key.

**Nudges.** Ask: "What happens if your coworker finishes first, takes their lock off and restarts the machine while you're still inside?" And: "Is a tag a physical barrier?"

**The answer.** Apply your own lock and tag, and verify zero energy, before you reach in. Trusting their lock leaves you unprotected if they remove it. Hanging only a tag is a warning, not a physical barrier; where a lock can be applied, apply your lock. The scene "Group lockout and shift change" teaches this. For group lockout boxes and shift changes, follow your site's written program.

## Español: cómo ayudar con las prácticas

Las cuatro prácticas de LOTO 101 **no cuentan para la calificación**. Cada una tiene un botón de **Pista**. Primero explica la idea, luego da una pista a la vez, y da la respuesta completa solo si el trabajador ya lo intentó y la vuelve a pedir.

- **Relacionar la energía con su dispositivo:** eléctrica → interruptor de desconexión con candado; neumática → válvula de aire con bloqueo y purga; vapor → válvula de compuerta con cubierta de bloqueo; gravedad → bloque mecánico o pasador.
- **Tocar los puntos de aislamiento verdaderos:** el interruptor de desconexión eléctrica y la válvula de cierre de aire con candado (con escape). El botón de paro de emergencia y la pantalla táctil son controles, no puntos de aislamiento (1910.147(b)).
- **Ordenar los siete pasos:** preparar (leer la placa), avisar a los empleados afectados, apagar con el paro normal, aislar cada fuente de energía, poner tu candado y tu etiqueta, liberar o bloquear la energía almacenada, verificar energía cero.
- **"Ya estás cubierto, yo bloqueé":** pon tu propio candado y tu etiqueta y verifica energía cero antes de meter las manos. Solo tu candado te protege.
