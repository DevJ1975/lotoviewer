# Contractors and outside servicing personnel (29 CFR 1910.147(f)(2))

## Both employers share their procedures

Whenever outside servicing personnel, such as contractors or OEM field technicians, do work covered by the standard, the on-site employer and the outside employer must **inform each other of their respective lockout or tagout procedures** (1910.147(f)(2)(i)).

## Site employees respect the contractor's program

The on-site employer must make sure its own employees understand and comply with the restrictions and prohibitions of the outside employer's energy control program (1910.147(f)(2)(ii)). If a contractor's lock or tag is on a machine, treat it exactly like your own site's: never remove it, operate around it or bypass it.

## Working side by side

When site employees and contractors work on the same equipment, use group lockout so that **every person, contractor or employee, has their own lock** on the group lockbox. Agree before the job starts on:

- who is the primary authorized employee coordinating the job (1910.147(f)(3)(ii)(C));
- how shift changes and handovers will work (1910.147(f)(4));
- how the site's permits (confined space, hot work) interact with the lockout.

## Contractor training

Contractors are responsible for training their own employees. Your site may still require contractors to complete site-specific orientation or a STRIKE module before a permit is issued. **Follow your site's contractor program.**
