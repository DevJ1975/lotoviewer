# The seven steps of applying lockout/tagout (29 CFR 1910.147(c)(9), (d))

Always follow your machine's written energy control procedure (the lockout placard). The standard's sequence comes down to seven steps.

## 1. Prepare for shutdown — 1910.147(d)(1)

Before you turn anything off, know:

- the type and magnitude of every energy source,
- the hazards of that energy,
- how to control it.

Read the placard. If the placard doesn't match the machine in front of you, stop and tell your supervisor.

## 2. Notify affected employees — 1910.147(c)(9)

Tell the operators and anyone working nearby that the machine is being shut down and locked out, and why.

## 3. Shut the machine down — 1910.147(d)(2)

Stop the machine in an orderly way using its **normal stopping procedure**: the stop button, cycle stop or switch. Shutting down properly avoids creating extra hazards, such as pulling a disconnect while a motor is under load.

## 4. Isolate every energy source — 1910.147(d)(3)

Find and operate **every** energy-isolating device so the machine is cut off from its energy: open the disconnects and close the valves. An E-stop or control button is not an isolation point.

## 5. Apply your lock and tag — 1910.147(d)(4)

Only authorized employees apply lockout or tagout devices (1910.147(d)(4)(i)).

- Lockout devices must hold the isolating devices in the "safe" or "off" position (1910.147(d)(4)(ii)).
- Each person working on the machine uses their **own** lock. One person, one lock, one key, and the key stays with you.
- Where a tag must be used, it goes at the same point a lock would have gone and must clearly show that operating the device is prohibited (1910.147(d)(4)(iii)–(iv)).

## 6. Release or restrain stored energy — 1910.147(d)(5)

After locks go on, relieve, disconnect, restrain or otherwise make safe all stored and residual energy:

- bleed air and hydraulic pressure,
- block or lower raised parts,
- let flywheels and blades come to a full stop,
- let hot parts cool,
- discharge capacitors per the procedure.

If energy could build up again, keep checking until the work is finished (1910.147(d)(5)(ii)).

## 7. Verify isolation — 1910.147(d)(6)

Before you start work, prove the machine is isolated and de-energized:

1. Make sure **everyone is clear** of the machine.
2. Try to start it with the normal controls. Nothing should happen.
3. Where the procedure requires a test for electrical energy, it must be done by a **qualified person** using proper test equipment. Under 29 CFR 1910.333(b)(2)(iv)(B), a qualified person tests the circuit elements and electrical parts to verify they are de-energized. If you are not a qualified electrical worker, do not do voltage tests yourself.
4. **Return the controls to off/neutral** so the machine won't start when the locks come off.

Only now is the machine at zero energy for your task. If anything about the verification is unclear, stop and ask your supervisor or the authorized employee in charge.

## Testing or positioning during the job — 1910.147(f)(1)

If the machine must be energized briefly to test or position it, follow this sequence:

1. Clear tools and materials.
2. Remove employees from the area.
3. Remove locks following the release rules.
4. Energize and test.
5. De-energize and re-apply all energy control before going back in.
