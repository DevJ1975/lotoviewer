# LOTO frequently asked questions (29 CFR 1910.147)

## English

**What's the difference between an authorized and an affected employee?**
An authorized employee locks out the machine and services it. An affected employee runs the machine or works nearby. Affected employees are told when locks go on and come off, but they don't apply locks for servicing (1910.147(b), (c)(9)).

**Can I clear a jam without locking out if it's quick?**
No, not unless your site's written program covers that exact task under the minor servicing exception with an alternative protective measure. Clearing a jam with your hands in the danger zone is servicing (1910.147(a)(2)(ii)). If in doubt, lock it out.

**Is pressing the E-stop enough?**
No. Push buttons, selector switches and other control-circuit devices are not energy-isolating devices (1910.147(b)). Lock the disconnects and valves on the placard.

**Can I use just a tag?**
Only where your employer's written procedure says that isolation point can't accept a lock, or where the employer has shown tagout gives full protection with extra measures (1910.147(c)(2), (c)(3)). It is not a field decision. Follow your site's written program.

**My coworker already locked the machine. Do I need my own lock?**
Yes. Every person working on the machine applies their own lock. Someone else's lock protects them, not you (1910.147(f)(3)(ii)(D)).

**What if the placard doesn't match the machine?**
Stop. Don't guess. Tell your supervisor so the procedure can be corrected before work starts (1910.147(d)(1)).

**How do I know the machine is really at zero energy?**
Release stored energy, make sure everyone is clear, try to start it with the normal controls, have a qualified person do any voltage test the procedure requires, then return the controls to off (1910.147(d)(5), (d)(6); 1910.333(b)(2)(iv)(B)).

**Can I test for voltage myself?**
Only if you are a qualified person for electrical work. Under 1910.333(b)(2)(iv)(B), a qualified person tests circuit elements to verify they are de-energized.

**What is stored energy?**
Energy that stays after the source is off: hydraulic and air pressure, springs, capacitors, coasting flywheels, heat, steam, and gravity on raised parts. It must be released or restrained (1910.147(d)(5)).

**What do I do at shift change?**
Follow your site's shift-change procedure. Usually the incoming worker locks on before the outgoing worker removes their lock, so protection never lapses (1910.147(f)(4)).

**Someone left their lock on and went home. Can I cut it off?**
No. Only the employer, following the site's written removal procedure, may remove it. That means confirming the person isn't on site, trying to contact them, and making sure they know before they return to work (1910.147(e)(3)).

**Who tells the operators the machine is back on?**
The authorized employee notifies affected employees after the locks are removed and before the machine is started (1910.147(e)(2)(ii)).

**Do I lock out to clean the line?**
If cleaning puts you in a danger zone or requires removing guards, yes. Sanitation is servicing when you are exposed to unexpected start-up (1910.147(b), (a)(2)(ii)).

**What about cord-and-plug equipment?**
Unplugging can be enough if the plug stays under your exclusive control while you work, for example in your hand or in a plug lockout (1910.147(a)(2)(iii)(A)).

**Can a machine be turned on briefly during the job to test it?**
Yes, but only with the testing sequence: clear tools, clear people, remove locks properly, test, then de-energize and re-lock before going back in (1910.147(f)(1)).

**Who can apply locks?**
Only trained authorized employees (1910.147(c)(8)).

**How often do I need LOTO training?**
Whenever your job, the equipment or the procedure changes, or an inspection shows you need it (1910.147(c)(7)(iii)). Many sites also require a yearly refresher. Follow your site's program.

**What does the annual inspection check?**
An authorized employee who doesn't use that procedure watches it being used and reviews responsibilities with the employees, at least once a year (1910.147(c)(6)).

**A contractor's lock is on the machine. What do I do?**
Treat it like any lock: leave it alone and don't operate the machine. Site employees must respect the contractor's energy control program (1910.147(f)(2)(ii)).

**What if I feel unsafe or something doesn't look right?**
Stop the work and get your supervisor. You never have to start servicing a machine you don't believe is safe.

**Does passing this module make me an authorized employee?**
It counts toward your training record, but your employer designates authorized employees and may require hands-on training on specific equipment (1910.147(c)(7)(iv)).

## Español

**¿Cuál es la diferencia entre un empleado autorizado y un empleado afectado?**
El empleado autorizado bloquea la máquina y le da servicio. El empleado afectado opera la máquina o trabaja cerca. Al empleado afectado se le avisa cuando se colocan y se quitan los candados, pero no coloca candados para el servicio (1910.147(b), (c)(9)).

**¿Puedo usar solo una etiqueta en lugar de un candado?**
Solo si el procedimiento escrito de su empleador dice que ese punto no acepta candado, o si el empleador demostró que el etiquetado da protección completa con medidas adicionales (1910.147(c)(3)). No es una decisión que se toma en el piso. Siga el programa escrito de su planta.

**¿Qué hago al cambio de turno?**
Siga el procedimiento de cambio de turno de su planta. Normalmente, el trabajador que entra pone su candado antes de que el que sale quite el suyo, para que la protección nunca se interrumpa (1910.147(f)(4)).

**Mi compañero ya puso su candado. ¿Necesito el mío?**
Sí. Cada persona que trabaja en la máquina pone su propio candado. El candado de otra persona la protege a ella, no a usted (1910.147(f)(3)(ii)(D)).

**¿Presionar el paro de emergencia es suficiente?**
No. Los botones y selectores son dispositivos de control, no dispositivos de aislamiento de energía (1910.147(b)). Bloquee los desconectores y válvulas que indica la placa.
