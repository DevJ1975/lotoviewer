# Purpose and scope of lockout/tagout (29 CFR 1910.147(a))

## What the standard is for

OSHA's standard for the control of hazardous energy, 29 CFR 1910.147, covers servicing and maintenance of machines and equipment where someone could be hurt by the **unexpected start-up or energization** of the machine, or by the **release of stored energy** (1910.147(a)(1)(i)). Lockout/tagout (LOTO) is how you make a machine safe before you put any part of your body where it could be hurt, and keep it safe until the work is done.

OSHA estimates that compliance with the standard prevents about 120 fatalities and 50,000 injuries each year.

## What counts as servicing and maintenance

Servicing and maintenance includes constructing, installing, setting up, adjusting, inspecting, modifying, maintaining and servicing machines. It also includes lubricating, cleaning, clearing jams and making tool changes when you could be exposed to unexpected energization or start-up (definition of "servicing and/or maintenance", 1910.147(b)).

In a food plant, that means sanitation and jam clearing are servicing when they put you in the danger zone.

## Normal production operations

Normal production work is covered by machine guarding (Subpart O), not LOTO, **unless** during production you must (1910.147(a)(2)(ii)):

- remove or bypass a guard or other safety device, or
- place any part of your body into the point of operation, or into a danger zone that exists during the machine's operating cycle.

If either is true, LOTO applies.

## The minor servicing exception

The standard has a narrow exception (Note to 1910.147(a)(2)(ii)). Minor tool changes, adjustments and other minor servicing during normal production are not covered **only if all of these are true**:

1. the task is routine, repetitive, and integral to using the equipment for production, **and**
2. the work is done using **alternative measures that provide effective protection**, such as a specifically designed tool, an interlock that truly prevents motion, or a written alternative procedure.

"It only takes a second" is not an alternative measure. If your site uses the minor servicing exception, it must be written into your site's program for that specific task. **Follow your site's written program.** If you are not sure a task qualifies, lock it out.

## What is not covered

The standard does not apply to (1910.147(a)(2)(iii)):

- work on cord-and-plug connected electric equipment when unplugging it removes the hazard **and** the plug stays under the exclusive control of the person doing the work (for example, the plug is in your hand or locked in a plug lockout);
- certain hot tap operations on pressurized pipelines that meet the standard's specific conditions.

## The three parts of an energy control program

Every employer must have an energy control program with three parts (1910.147(c)(1)):

1. written **energy control procedures** for each machine (your LOTO placard is the field version);
2. **training** for every employee who works on or around the equipment;
3. **periodic inspections** of the procedures, at least once a year.
