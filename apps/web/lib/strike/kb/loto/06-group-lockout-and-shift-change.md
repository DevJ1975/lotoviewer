# Group lockout and shift change (29 CFR 1910.147(f)(3), (f)(4))

## Group lockout — 1910.147(f)(3)

When a crew works on the same machine, a group lockout must give **each person the same protection as a personal lock** (1910.147(f)(3)(i)). A common method:

1. One authorized employee (the primary authorized employee) shuts down, isolates and locks out the machine, and verifies zero energy.
2. The keys to those locks go into a **group lockbox**.
3. **Every worker adds their own personal lock** to the lockbox before starting work, and removes it when they stop working on the job (1910.147(f)(3)(ii)(D)).
4. The lockbox can't be opened, and the machine can't be re-energized, until every personal lock is off.

The standard also requires (1910.147(f)(3)(ii)(A)–(C)):

- primary responsibility is given to one authorized employee for the set of people working under the group lock;
- that authorized employee must be able to find out the exposure status of each person in the group;
- when more than one crew, craft or department is involved, one authorized employee is designated to coordinate the overall job.

**You are never protected by someone else's lock.** If a coworker says "you're covered, I locked it out", put your own lock on anyway.

## Shift and personnel changes — 1910.147(f)(4)

When a job carries over from one shift to the next, the site must have specific procedures that keep lockout/tagout protection **continuous**, including an orderly transfer between the people leaving and the people arriving.

The usual way to do this is **"lock on before lock off"**: the incoming worker applies their lock before the outgoing worker removes theirs, so the machine is never left without protection. Follow your site's shift-change procedure for the exact steps, including how the primary authorized employee role is handed over.

## Contractors on a group job

When outside contractors are part of the work, the site and the contractor must share their LOTO procedures with each other (1910.147(f)(2)). See the contractors topic.
