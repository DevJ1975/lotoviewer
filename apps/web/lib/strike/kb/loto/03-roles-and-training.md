# Roles and training (29 CFR 1910.147(b), (c)(7), (c)(8))

## Authorized employee

An authorized employee is the person who **locks or tags out** machines or equipment in order to service them (definition in 1910.147(b)). Only authorized employees may perform energy isolation (1910.147(c)(8)).

Training for authorized employees covers (1910.147(c)(7)(i)(A)):

- recognizing the hazardous energy sources,
- the type and magnitude of the energy in the workplace,
- the methods and means needed to isolate and control that energy.

## Affected employee

An affected employee **operates or uses** a machine on which servicing is done under LOTO, or works in an area where that servicing is being done (definition in 1910.147(b)). Affected employees do not apply locks for servicing. They must be trained in the purpose and use of the energy control procedure (1910.147(c)(7)(i)(B)), and they must be notified before locks go on and after they come off (1910.147(c)(9)).

**Important:** if your job ever requires you to reach into a machine, for example to clear a jam or clean it, that is servicing. You must be trained as an authorized employee and lock out first, unless your site's written program covers the task under the minor servicing exception.

## Other employees

All other employees who work in an area where energy control procedures may be used must be told about the procedure. They must also know they are **prohibited from trying to restart or re-energize** a machine that is locked or tagged out (1910.147(c)(7)(i)(C)).

## Tagout training

Where tags are used, employees must also learn the limits of tags (1910.147(c)(7)(ii)):

- a tag is a warning, not a physical restraint;
- tags must never be removed without authorization, ignored or bypassed;
- tags must be legible and securely attached;
- tags can create a false sense of security.

## When retraining is required

Retraining is required (1910.147(c)(7)(iii)) when there is:

- a change in your job assignment,
- a change in machines, equipment or processes that brings a new hazard,
- a change in the energy control procedures, or
- a periodic inspection, or the employer's own observations, showing deviations or gaps in your knowledge.

OSHA does not set one fixed refresher interval for LOTO training. Many sites require a yearly refresher. **Follow your site's program.**

## Certification

The employer must certify that training was completed and keep it current. The certification includes each employee's name and the dates of training (1910.147(c)(7)(iv)). Passing this STRIKE module is part of your site's training record. It does not by itself make you an authorized employee; your employer designates that.
