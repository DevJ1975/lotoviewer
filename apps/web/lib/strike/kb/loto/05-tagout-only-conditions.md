# When a tag may be used instead of a lock (29 CFR 1910.147(c)(2), (c)(3), (c)(5))

## Lockout is the default

If an energy-isolating device **can** be locked out, the employer must use lockout. The only exception is where the employer can show that a tagout program provides full employee protection (1910.147(c)(2)(ii)). Since January 2, 1990, new equipment and equipment that is replaced, majorly repaired, renovated or modified must be designed to accept a lockout device (1910.147(c)(2)(iii)).

Whether a specific isolation point uses tag-only is a decision your employer makes and writes into the procedure. **It is never a worker's call in the field.** Follow your site's written program and the machine's placard.

## Full employee protection with tags — 1910.147(c)(3)

When tags are used on a device that could be locked, the tag must:

- go at the same place the lock would have gone, and
- be part of a program that gives a level of safety equivalent to lockout.

The employer must add **at least one more safety measure**, such as:

- removing an isolating circuit element,
- blocking a controlling switch,
- opening an extra disconnecting device, or
- removing a valve handle.

## What a tag must be — 1910.147(c)(5)

- **Durable**: it holds up in the environment, including wet, damp or corrosive areas, and the print doesn't wash out.
- **Standardized**: color, shape or size, and print and format are standardized at the site.
- **Substantial**: it is attached with a non-reusable, self-locking, hand-attachable fastener with an unlocking strength of no less than 50 pounds, equivalent to a one-piece all-environment-tolerant nylon cable tie.
- **Identifiable**: it shows who applied it.
- **Warning**: it carries a message such as "Do Not Start", "Do Not Open", "Do Not Close", "Do Not Energize" or "Do Not Operate".

## The limits of a tag

A tag is a warning, not a physical barrier (1910.147(c)(7)(ii)). It does not stop someone from flipping a switch or opening a valve. Never remove, ignore or bypass someone else's tag. If you see a tag that looks wrong or unreadable, stop and tell your supervisor.
