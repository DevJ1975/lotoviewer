# Energy types and energy-isolating devices (29 CFR 1910.147(b), (d)(3))

## Energy sources on your placard

Your machine's lockout placard lists every energy source and its isolation point. Each source has a colored code:

| Code | Energy | Typical isolation |
|---|---|---|
| E | Electrical | Lockable disconnect switch or circuit breaker, in the OFF position |
| G | Gas (fuel gas lines) | Lockable gas shutoff valve, closed |
| H | Hydraulic | Shut the pump off, lock the valve, bleed the pressure, block the cylinder |
| P | Pneumatic | Lockable air shutoff valve with exhaust; bleed the line |
| M | Mechanical | Let flywheels and blades stop; block or pin moving parts |
| T | Thermal | Isolate heat source; let surfaces cool before touching |
| W | Water | Lockable valve, closed and drained where needed |
| S | Steam | Lockable gate valve (valve cover lock), closed and bled |
| V | Valve | A process valve step listed on the placard; lock it in the position shown |
| CG | Compressed gas | Lockable cylinder or line valve, closed and vented |
| CP | Control panel | A step at the panel; read the placard carefully (see below) |
| GR | Gravity | Lower the part, or block, pin or chain it so it cannot fall |

Most machines have more than one energy source. Every source on the placard must be controlled before work begins (1910.147(d)(3)).

## What an energy-isolating device is

An energy-isolating device is a **mechanical device that physically prevents the transmission or release of energy** (definition in 1910.147(b)). Examples:

- a manually operated electrical circuit breaker,
- a disconnect switch,
- a manually operated switch that disconnects all ungrounded supply conductors, where no pole can be operated independently,
- a line valve,
- a block,
- any similar device used to block or isolate energy.

## What is NOT an energy-isolating device

**Push buttons, selector switches and other control-circuit devices are not energy-isolating devices** (1910.147(b)). That includes:

- the emergency stop (E-stop) button,
- a start/stop station,
- an operator touchscreen or HMI,
- a PLC "safe mode".

These control the machine. They do not cut off its energy. A fault, a coworker or a program can override them. If your placard lists a CP (control panel) step, follow it exactly. It never replaces locking the real isolation points.

## Stored and residual energy

Turning off the source does not remove energy that is already stored. Examples include:

- hydraulic accumulators and pressurized cylinders,
- air trapped in lines and receivers,
- springs under tension,
- coasting flywheels and blades,
- capacitors,
- hot surfaces and steam traps,
- raised forks, platens and hoppers (gravity).

All stored energy must be relieved, disconnected, restrained or otherwise made safe (1910.147(d)(5)). If energy could build up again, keep verifying until the work is finished (1910.147(d)(5)(ii)).

## Lockout hardware

Locks, tags, hasps, valve covers, blocks and chains must be supplied by the employer. They must be **durable, standardized, substantial and identifiable**, and used only for energy control (1910.147(c)(5)). Your lock and tag must identify you (1910.147(c)(5)(ii)(D)).
